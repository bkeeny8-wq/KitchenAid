Roasted Garlic & Gruyere Mashed Potatoes - serves 4
Active 35 min, total about 1 hr 25 min (the garlic is most of it)

GARLIC - can be done ahead
2 whole heads garlic
1 tbsp olive oil

POTATOES
2 lb Yukon Gold, peeled, cut into 1 1/2 inch chunks
1 tbsp kosher salt, for the water

TO FINISH
5 tbsp unsalted butter - 2 for drying, 3 for the cream
3/4 cup heavy cream
4 oz Gruyere, grated on the big holes
1/2 tsp kosher salt
1/4 tsp white pepper
pinch nutmeg

1. Oven to 400F. Sit the whole heads on foil, drizzle with the oil, wrap
   loosely, roast 45-50 min until a head gives when you press it. Cool a
   little, then squeeze each head from the root end straight into the bowl.
   Nothing needs peeling.

2. Potatoes into a big pot, cover with cold water by an inch, add the 1 tbsp
   salt. Boil 18-20 min, until a knife meets no resistance at all.

3. Drain and let them sit a moment to stop dripping.

4. Empty pot back on low heat, 2 tbsp butter in, potatoes in. Turn them gently
   2-3 min until the steam thins out and a faint floury film shows on the
   bottom of the pot. That film is how you know they are dry.

5. Meanwhile warm the cream with the other 3 tbsp butter until it steams.
   Do not let it boil.

6. Rice them or mash by hand back into the pot. No food processor or stick
   blender - it turns to glue in seconds.

7. Fold in the warm cream and butter in two or three additions. Stop while it
   still holds a soft shape.

8. Off the heat, fold in the squeezed garlic, then the Gruyere a handful at a
   time until smooth.

9. Season now - salt, white pepper, nutmeg if you want it.

THREE THINGS THAT MATTER
- The floury film in step 4 is the whole recipe. Water you leave in the potato
  is butter you cannot add later.
- The Gruyere goes in off the heat. Above about 160F it tightens, squeezes its
  fat out and goes stringy, and there is no way back.
- Season last. The cheese and the cooking water have both already salted it.
