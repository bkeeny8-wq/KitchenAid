 Fluffy Whipped Egg White Pancakes
 Base recipe with spice mix-ins. Compotes follow, one per page. Makes about 8 pancakes, serves 4.



 Pancake Batter
    1 1/2 cups           all-purpose flour (190g), spooned and leveled, not scooped

    2 tbsp               sugar

    2 tsp                baking powder (do not skip)

    1/2 tsp              kosher salt

    1 1/4 cups           buttermilk, or whole milk

    2                    large eggs, separated

    3 tbsp               unsalted butter, melted, plus more for the pan

    1 tsp                vanilla extract

    1/4 tsp              cream of tartar (optional, helps the whites hold)

 Spice Mix-Ins (add to the dry ingredients)
    Cinnamon             1/2 tsp, good with the apple compote

    Nutmeg               1/4 tsp, freshly grated if possible

    Both                 1/4 tsp cinnamon plus 1/8 tsp nutmeg for a lighter touch

 Method
 1. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, salt, and any spices together in a large bowl.
 2. In a second bowl, whisk the buttermilk, egg yolks, melted butter, and vanilla. Pour into the dry
     ingredients and stir just until combined. A few lumps are fine. If the batter looks very thick, loosen it
     with a splash of milk.
 3. In a tall, clean, dry container, whip the egg whites with the cream of tartar using an immersion blender
     (whisk attachment works best) until stiff, glossy peaks form, about 3 minutes.
 4. Stir a third of the whites into the batter to loosen it, then fold in the rest with a spatula in two additions.
    Stop while a few white streaks remain. Rest the batter 5 minutes.
 5. Heat a nonstick pan or griddle over medium-low and lightly butter it.
 6. Scoop about 1/3 cup batter per pancake and let it mound tall. Cook until bubbles form and the edges
    look set, then flip once and cook until golden and cooked through, about 4 minutes total.
 7. Serve right away, or hold on a rack in a 200 F oven while you finish the batches.

 Fluff checklist: use fresh baking powder, weigh or spoon the flour, keep the whites stiff, fold gently, keep the
 heat at medium-low, and do not press the pancakes after flipping.

 Blueberry Compote
 Summer. Spoon warm over the pancakes. Keeps covered in the fridge up to 5 days.


 Ingredients
    2 cups               blueberries (300g), fresh or frozen

    2 to 3 tbsp          maple syrup, adjusted to the sweetness of the berries

    1 tbsp               lemon juice

    1 tbsp               water

    1 pinch              kosher salt

 Method
 1. Combine everything in a small saucepan over medium heat.
 2. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until about half the berries
    have burst and the juices turn syrupy.
 3. Cool 5 minutes. It thickens as it sits.

 Tip: mash a few berries against the side of the pan for a thicker, jammier sauce.

 Mixed Berry Compote
 Summer. Spoon warm over the pancakes. Keeps covered in the fridge up to 5 days.


 Ingredients
    2 cups               mixed berries (300g): any mix of strawberries, blueberries, raspberries, and
                         blackberries, with strawberries hulled and quartered

    2 to 3 tbsp          maple syrup, adjusted to the sweetness of the berries

    1 tbsp               lemon juice

    1 tbsp               water

    1/2 tsp              vanilla extract, stirred in at the end

    1 pinch              kosher salt

 Method
 1. Combine the berries, maple syrup, lemon juice, water, and salt in a small saucepan over medium
    heat.
 2. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the berries soften and the juices reduce to a loose
    syrup.
 3. Off the heat, stir in the vanilla. Serve warm.

 Tip: add raspberries in the last 2 minutes since they break down fastest, and stop before everything turns to
 mush if you want visible pieces.

 Apple Compote
 Autumn. Spoon warm over the pancakes. Keeps covered in the fridge up to 5 days.


 Ingredients
    3 medium             apples (about 1 lb or 450g), peeled and diced 1/2 inch

    2 to 3 tbsp          maple syrup

    1 tbsp               unsalted butter

    1 tbsp               lemon juice

    1/4 cup              water or apple cider

    1/2 tsp              cinnamon

    1 pinch              nutmeg

    1 pinch              kosher salt

 Method
 1. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the apples and maple syrup and cook 2
    minutes.
 2. Add the lemon juice, water or cider, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt. Cover and simmer 8 to 10 minutes,
    stirring occasionally, until the apples are tender.
 3. Uncover and cook 2 more minutes to thicken the juices. Serve warm.

 Tip: firm apples like Honeycrisp, Granny Smith, or Braeburn hold their shape best.
