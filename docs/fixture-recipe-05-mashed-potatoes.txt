Roasted Garlic and Gruyere Mashed Potatoes
Yukon Golds dried in the pot with butter, then loosened with warm cream. Roasted garlic and
Gruyere folded in off the heat.

 Makes                                            Active time                        Total time
 6 cups (serves 4)                                About 35 minutes                   About 1 hour 25 minutes

Ingredients
Roasted garlic (can be made ahead)
   2 heads                  garlic, left whole
      1 tbsp                        olive oil
Potatoes
   2 lb                             Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into 1 1/2 inch chunks
      1 tbsp                        kosher salt, for the cooking water
To finish
    5 tbsp                          unsalted butter, 2 tbsp for drying and 3 tbsp for the cream
      3/4 cup                       heavy cream
      4 oz                          Gruyere, grated on the large holes
      1/2 tsp                       kosher salt
      1/4 tsp                       white pepper

Instructions
 1.    Roast the garlic (50 min). Heat the oven to 400F. Sit the whole heads on a square of foil,
       drizzle with the olive oil, wrap loosely and roast 45 to 50 minutes, until a head gives when
       you press it and smells sweet rather than sharp. Cool until you can handle them, then
       squeeze each head from the root end straight into the mixing bowl. The cloves come out on
       their own and nothing needs peeling.
 2.    Boil the potatoes (20 min). Put the potatoes in a large pot, cover with cold water by an
       inch and add the 1 tbsp of salt. Bring to a boil, then cook 18 to 20 minutes until a knife
       slides into a chunk with no resistance at all. Start from cold water so the outsides do not
       blow apart before the middles are done.
 3.    Drain them properly (2 min). Drain in a colander and leave them a moment to stop dripping.
       Water left in the potato is butter you cannot add later, so this is not a step to rush.
 4.    Dry them in the pot (3 min). Put the empty pot back on low heat, add 2 tbsp of the butter
       and tip the potatoes in. Turn them gently for 2 to 3 minutes until the steam thins out and a
       faint floury film shows on the bottom of the pot. That film is the sign they are dry and
       ready to take the cream.
 5.    Warm the cream (3 min). While the potatoes dry, heat the cream with the remaining 3 tbsp
       butter until it steams but does not boil. Cold cream cools the potatoes and stiffens the
       starch, and you end up beating the mash to get it smooth.

 6.    Mash (5 min). Pass the potatoes through a ricer back into the warm pot, or mash by hand. Do
       not use a food processor or a stick blender: the blades shear the starch granules open and
       the mash turns to glue in seconds.
 7.    Add the cream (3 min). Pour in the warm cream and butter in two or three additions, folding
       with a spatula rather than beating. Stop while it still holds a soft shape on the spoon.
 8.    Fold in the garlic and the Gruyere. Take the pot off the heat completely. Fold in the
       squeezed roasted garlic, which breaks up on its own as it goes through, then the Gruyere a
       handful at a time until it is melted and the mash is smooth. Residual heat only: above about
       160F the cheese tightens, squeezes out its fat and goes stringy.
 9.    Season and serve. Salt and white pepper. Taste now and not before, because the Gruyere and
       the cooking water have both already salted it.

Notes
 •    Yukon Gold mashes buttery and holds its shape. Russets go fluffier and drier and will drink more
      cream, so add it a little at a time if you use them.
 •    The garlic keeps three days in the fridge under a film of oil, and roasting the heads while the
      oven is on for something else is the easiest way to get this on the table in 35 minutes.
 •    Folding the Gruyere off the heat is not fussiness. Hard alpine cheeses break above roughly 160F,
      and a broken mash is greasy and stringy with no way back.
 •    To hold it before serving, cover and set the pot over a pan of barely simmering water, then stir
      in a splash more warm cream just before it goes out.
 •    Grate the Gruyere on the large holes. Finely grated cheese clumps as it hits the heat; larger
      shreds melt into the mash more evenly.
